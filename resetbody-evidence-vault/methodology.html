The audit standard

Clear verdicts.
Honest uncertainty.

A verdict is a practical interpretation of the available evidence, not a replacement for the underlying studies. We show the finding, the population it applies to, and what remains uncertain.

01 / Verdicts

Three ways to interpret a claim.

Verdicts describe the likely direction of an effect in the stated context. They are not moral labels and do not guarantee an individual outcome.

↑Protects

Supports retention

Evidence suggests the practice can help preserve muscle, strength, or the conditions that support them during fat loss.

↔Neutral

No established universal edge

The effect is small, inconsistent, uncertain, or mostly dependent on context. Neutral does not mean proven identical for everyone.

↓Costs

Potential trade-off

Evidence suggests a choice may undermine training quality, recovery, or muscle retention in the stated context.

02 / Certainty

How sure are we?

Certainty is assessed separately from verdict direction. A plausible benefit can still have limited evidence behind it.

LabelMeaningWhat to expect
StrongConsistent, reasonably direct evidence with few major concerns for the stated conclusion.Useful as a general principle, while individual response can vary.
ModerateEvidence supports the conclusion, but indirectness, variation, or meaningful limitations remain.Likely useful in context; avoid treating it as a universal rule.
LimitedSparse, inconsistent, indirect, or low-confidence evidence.Do not rely on it as a firm recommendation; more research may change the picture.
03 / Process

How a claim gets audited.

Each published claim should be traceable from the everyday question to the underlying source and the practical conclusion.

Define the claim precisely

Clarify what is being claimed, for whom, under what conditions, and which outcome matters — for example, strength, lean mass, or scale weight.

Inspect the best available evidence

Prioritize relevant systematic reviews, meta-analyses, randomized trials, and well-designed observational research as appropriate. Study design alone does not guarantee quality.

Check population and limitations

Record study design, participants, intervention, duration, outcomes, uncertainty, and whether findings reasonably apply to the claim.

Separate finding from advice

Summarize what was observed, state what is not established, then write a plain-English takeaway that does not exceed the evidence.

Assign verdict and certainty

Document the reasoning behind both labels. Update the audit when better evidence appears or a citation or interpretation is corrected.
